**This is typically not a hardware defect. The 'Liquid Glass' design language introduced in iPadOS 26 utilizes heavy 'Frosted Glass' and 'Depth of Field' effects, which can be mistaken for a foggy or out-of-focus screen.**

## How to Distinguish Design from Defect:

### 1. The Screenshot Test (Quick Diagnosis)

- Have the student take a screenshot (Power + Volume Up).
- View the screenshot in the **Photos** app:
  - **Software Intent**: If the screenshot itself contains blurry backgrounds (e.g., the Lock Screen), it is a **system-level design effect**. The hardware is functional.
  - **Hardware Defect**: If the screenshot appears sharp when sent to another device, but the iPad screen remains physically blurry, the **display panel** or coating may be failing.

### 2. Adjust Liquid Glass Settings

- Navigate to **[Settings]** > **[Display & Brightness]** > **[Appearance]**.
- Check the "Liquid Glass Transparency" levels. High settings significantly increase background blur.

## How to Resolve:

- **If the student finds it distracting**:
  - Go to **[Settings]** > **[Accessibility]** > **[Display & Text Size]**.
  - Toggle **"Reduce Transparency"** to ON. This disables Liquid Glass effects and reverts to solid backgrounds, improving legibility.
- **If it is a confirmed hardware issue**:
  - If the screenshot is sharp but the physical screen shows persistent fuzziness or ghosting, and a restart does not fix it, submit a repair request.

## Expert Strategy:

Before rolling out major OS updates, proactive communication with teaching staff is key. Informing teachers about the iPadOS 26 visual refresh will prevent a surge of false hardware repair reports, streamlining administrative workflows.
