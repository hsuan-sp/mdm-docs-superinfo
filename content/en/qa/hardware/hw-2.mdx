**When an iPad fails to charge, we recommend a "Systematic Cross-Testing" protocol to precisely isolate whether the fault lies with the iPad hardware, the charging cable, or the cart’s power distribution module. This methodical approach clarifies maintenance responsibilities, ensuring rapid warranty replacements and accurate budgeting for out-of-warranty repairs.**

## Cross-Testing Workflow:

### Stage 1: Slot and Cable Test

1. Move the non-charging iPad to a **known working slot** in the cart (one that successfully charged another iPad).
2. Wait for 1 minute. Does the "Lightning bolt" icon appear in the status bar?
   - ✅ **If it charges**: The original cable or the power brick for that specific slot is likely faulty.
   - ❌ **If it still doesn't charge**: Move to Stage 2.

### Stage 2: Independent Power Test (Isolate Cart Variable)

1. Use a **certified MFi cable** and a **standalone 20W power adapter** (direct to a wall outlet).
2. Connect it directly to a wall power outlet.
   - ✅ **If it charges**: The issue lies within the cart’s internal power distribution unit (PDU) or a tripped breaker.
   - ❌ **If it still doesn't charge**: The issue is highly likely a **hardware failure on the iPad**.

### Stage 3: Physical Inspection

- **Port Check**: Inspect the Lightning or USB-C port for lint, dust, or debris. Carefully clear it using a non-conductive tool or compressed air. Never use metal pins.
- **Deep Discharge**: If an iPad has been unused for a long period, the battery voltage may be critically low. Keep it connected to a wall charger for at least **30-60 minutes** to wake the system.

## Expert Strategy:

- **Warranty Claim Protocol**: If cross-testing confirms a hardware fault (e.g., a damaged internal charging IC), immediately log the serial number and initiate an official repair request.
- **Consumable Budgeting**: Charging cables are high-attrition consumables in a classroom environment. Schools should allocate an annual budget to maintain a 5-10% reserve of MFi-certified replacement cables.
- **The Criticality of MFi**: Utilizing non-certified, substandard cables in the high-load, continuous-draw environment of a charging cart frequently causes catastrophic damage to the iPad's mainboard. IT must strictly regulate the sourcing and quality of all deployed cables.
