**The iPad 9th gen (released in 2021) has entered the latter stage of its hardware lifecycle after several years of campus service. While it currently supports iPadOS 26, schools face hardware aging and performance bottlenecks as software demands increase.**

## Fleet Evaluation Standards:

### 1. Performance vs. Modern Pedagogy

- **Advantage**: iPadOS 26 still supports iPad 9th gen, ensuring that security patches and MDM management commands remain operational.
- **Disadvantage**: The A13 chip with only 3GB of RAM causes noticeable lag when multitasking or using advanced AI-driven educational tools.

### 2. Battery & Storage Health

- **Battery Aging**: Most units are expected to have dipped below 80% maximum capacity, making them unreliable for a full continuous school day.
- **Storage**: 64GB has become the minimum requirement for 2026; students will likely face persistent "Storage Almost Full" warnings.

## Renewal vs. Replacement Indicators:

### Suitable for Continued Use (Tier 2):

- **Single-Purpose Tools**: E-readers, simple quiz stations, or language listening exercises.
- **Kiosks**: Library search stations or administrative attendance scanners.

### Recommended for Replacement:

- **Creative Arts**: The A13 struggles with modern 4K video editing and high-resolution graphic design.
- **Advanced Labs**: Cannot run the latest Apple Intelligence models or complex AR tracking environments.

## Transition Strategy:

1. **Redeployment**: Move aging iPad 9th gen units to "light duty" carts for standardized testing or basic research.
2. **Final Warranty Audit**: Before the warranty coverage period ends, identify all units with significant battery degradation for a final round of warranty repairs.
3. **Strategic Procurement**: Schools should prioritize purchasing M-series iPads (with at least 8GB of memory) to serve as primary devices for creative and explorative learning.
