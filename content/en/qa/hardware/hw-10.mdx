**Apple Pencil connectivity or input issues can generally be resolved by executing a re-pairing sequence, tightening the physical tip, or replenishing the battery. However, because different generations utilize distinct pairing and charging protocols, you must first verify your specific iPad model.**

In the current Apple ecosystem, there are four main versions of the Apple Pencil. Use the matrix below to verify hardware compatibility:

| Pencil Model               | Pairing / Charging Method                       | Compatible iPad Examples                  |
| :------------------------- | :---------------------------------------------- | :---------------------------------------- |
| **Apple Pencil (1st Gen)** | Plug into Lightning port (or use USB-C adapter) | iPad 9th gen, iPad (A16)                  |
| **Apple Pencil (2nd Gen)** | Magnetic attachment to side                     | iPad mini 6, iPad Air 4/5                 |
| **Apple Pencil (USB-C)**   | Pair and charge via USB-C cable                 | Any USB-C iPad (incl. M4 Pro/M2 Air)      |
| **Apple Pencil Pro**       | Magnetic attachment to side                     | iPad Pro (M4), iPad Air (M2), iPad mini 7 |

## Standard Troubleshooting Steps:

### 1. Check Battery Level

- **1st Gen**: Plug directly into the iPad.
- **2nd Gen / Pro**: Attach magnetically to the side connector.
- **USB-C**: Connect via a USB-C cable.
- **Verification**: Wait 10 minutes and check the "Batteries" widget on the iPad to ensure the charge is increasing.

### 2. Tighten the Tip (Nib)

A loose tip is the leading cause of intermittent writing. Gently twist the nib **clockwise** to ensure it is secure. If the metal pin inside the nib is visible, replace the tip immediately.

### 3. Re-pairing Workflow

1. Go to **[Settings]** > **[Bluetooth]**.
2. Find "Apple Pencil" in the "My Devices" list.
3. Tap the **(i)** icon > **[Forget This Device]**.
4. Re-connect the Pencil to the iPad (via magnet or cable) and tap "Pair" on the screen.

### 4. Screen Protector Interference

Some heavy-duty or matte screen protectors can reduce stylus sensitivity. If the Pencil works on another iPad but not yours, the screen protector may be the culprit.

## Expert Strategy:

The internal lithium-ion batteries in Apple Pencils are extremely small and highly susceptible to permanent degradation. If left completely uncharged for more than three months (such as during summer recess), the battery will enter an irreversible "Deep Discharge" state, rendering the pencil permanently inoperable. As device warranties inevitably expire, lost or irreparably damaged Apple Pencils will require out-of-pocket replacement by the school. Implementing strict charging and storage protocols is vital for maximizing the lifecycle of these high-value accessories and minimizing unnecessary operational expenditures.
