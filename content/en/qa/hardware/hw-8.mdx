**Yes. According to Apple's official guidelines, you can use 70% Isopropyl Alcohol wipes, 75% Ethyl Alcohol wipes, or disinfectant wipes on the hard, non-porous surfaces of the iPad.**

In a shared classroom environment, a proper disinfection routine is essential. Here is the Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) based on official recommendations:

## 1. Approved Cleaning Agents:

- **70% Isopropyl Alcohol (IPA) wipes**.
- **75% Ethyl Alcohol wipes**.
- **Clorox Disinfecting Wipes**.
- **A soft, lint-free microfiber cloth** dampened with water (for basic cleaning).

## 2. Prohibited Substances & Tools:

- ❌ **Bleach** or products containing **Hydrogen Peroxide**.
- ❌ **Window cleaners or household detergents** (these can strip the screen coating).
- ❌ **Aerosols, solvents, or abrasives**.
- ❌ **Compressed air** (can damage delicate microphone or speaker membranes).
- ❌ **Paper towels or napkins** (may cause micro-scratches).

## 3. Standard Cleaning Steps:

1. **Power Down**: Unplug all cables and accessories (like Apple Pencil) and ensure the iPad is fully turned off.
2. **Spray the Cloth**: **Never spray liquid directly onto the iPad.** Spray the alcohol onto a lint-free microfiber cloth until it is "slightly damp."
3. **Wipe Gently**: Avoid openings such as the charging port, speakers, or microphone holes to prevent liquid from seeping into the internal hardware.
4. **Accessory Care**: You can wipe the body of an Apple Pencil with a damp cloth, but **do not wipe the tip itself** or allow liquid into the connector.

## 4. Technical Risk Warnings:

- **Oleophobic Coating**: iPad screens feature an oil-resistant coating that naturally wears over time. Frequent or excessive use of alcohol will accelerate the degradation of this coating.
- **Nano-texture Glass**: If your high-end iPad is equipped with **Nano-texture glass**, only use the dedicated polishing cloth provided by Apple.
- **Fabric/Leather Accessories**: Avoid using alcohol on official Apple leather, Smart Folio fabrics, or keyboard cases, as it will cause material discoloration.

## Expert Strategy:

Liquid damage is never covered under Apple's standard limited warranty and is strictly classified as accidental/user damage. As devices age out of their initial warranty periods, proper preventative maintenance becomes even more critical to safeguarding the school's technology investments. We strongly advise that IT administrators or homeroom teachers strictly manage and dispense cleaning supplies, ensuring younger students do not accidentally spray liquids directly into device ports, which frequently causes catastrophic logic board failure.
