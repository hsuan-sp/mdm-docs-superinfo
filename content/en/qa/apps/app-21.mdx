**Apple introduced privacy features in iOS 18 allowing users to lock or hide specific apps via Face ID or passcode. In an educational environment, however, this can interfere with asset inventory and classroom management.**

## MDM Countermeasures (Requires Jamf Pro 11.9+ and iOS/iPadOS 18+)

Administrators should check the following two new restrictions within the **[Restrictions]** profile for **"Supervised"** devices:

1. **Don't Allow Locked Apps (allowLockedApps)**:
   - Enabling this restriction prevents users from applying Face ID/Touch ID authentication locks to any app.
   - **Note**: If this permission is disabled, the system will automatically disable the "Hide App" feature as well, because hiding requires locking first.

2. **Don't Allow Hidden Apps (allowHiddenApps)**:
   - Prevents users from moving apps into the system-level "Hidden" folder.

## Technical Implementation

In iOS/iPadOS 18 and later, these restrictions can be deployed via the _Configurations_ payload of **Declarative Device Management (DDM)**, making them take effect much faster than traditional profiles. Once applied, when a student long-presses an app icon on the Home Screen, the "Require Face ID" or "Hide and Require Face ID" options will be grayed out or removed, ensuring educational apps remain visible and accessible.
