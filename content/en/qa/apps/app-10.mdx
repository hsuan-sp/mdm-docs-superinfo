**This frustrating issue typically occurs because the app's internal "License Source" fundamentally conflicts with the deployment identity expected by the MDM. When the OS cannot verify a silent institutional license, it falls back to demanding the original purchaser's password.**

On stringently managed school iPads, these relentless "password pop-ups" are almost universally caused by one of three technical scenarios:

## Cause 1: Personal vs. Organizational License Conflict (Failed "Takeover")

- **The Scenario**: A student bypassed restrictions and manually downloaded YouTube from the App Store using their personal `@gmail.com` Apple Account. Months later, the IT Director uses Jamf Pro to deploy YouTube campus-wide to bring it under management.
- **The Mechanics**: Although the app binary is identical, the OS kernel flags the existing YouTube app as possessing a "Personal Consumer License." When the MDM attempts to silently convert it into a **"Managed App,"** if the takeover handshake fails (or if the student hits "Cancel"), the app remains personally licensed. Consequently, every time that app needs an update, the system will demand the password for the original `@gmail.com` account.
- **The Ultimate Fix**: **"Delete and Redeploy."** Instruct the student or teacher to hard-delete the app from the iPad springboard. Then, have them open the **[Self Service]** app and redownload it. This obliterates the personal license cache and forces a clean installation utilizing the school's silent VPP device license.

## Cause 2: Incorrect MDM Assignment Mode

- **The Scenario**: While configuring the app payload in Jamf Pro, the administrator accidentally selected **[User Assignment]** instead of Device Assignment in the VPP tab.
- **The Mechanics**: User Assignment requires a valid Apple Account to "hold" the digital license. If the iPad is operating in a shared, logged-out state, the OS will continuously spam the screen with login prompts trying to satisfy the license requirement.
- **The Fix**: Immediately log into Jamf Pro, navigate to the app's settings, and switch the distribution method to **[Device Assignment]**, binding the license directly to the hardware serial number.

## Cause 3: VPP Server Handshake Timeout or Token Expiry

- **The Scenario**: The MDM successfully pushed the app, but Apple's VPP licensing servers were congested and failed to register the device's serial number in time.
- **The Mechanics**: The iPad downloaded the app payload, but during the final cryptographic check, it found no valid institutional license token. The anti-piracy mechanisms trigger, requesting an Apple Account password to validate ownership.
- **The Fix**: Wait 10 minutes, or execute a **[Blank Push]** command from Jamf Pro to force the iPad to re-authenticate its VPP status with Apple's servers.

> **Important**: If apps **suddenly prompt for passwords campus-wide**, the most likely root cause is an **expired VPP Server Token**, not an individual device issue. IT Directors must codify "Annual ASM Token Renewal" into their standard operating procedures to prevent this.
