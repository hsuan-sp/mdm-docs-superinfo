**No. This will only result in the catastrophic loss of all local user data, and the device will be forcibly re-enrolled into the school's MDM immediately upon reconnecting to the internet. Hardware ownership and top-level management authority are permanently bound within the Apple School Manager (ASM) cloud architecture, and they cannot be erased by wiping the local OS.**

Students often mistakenly believe that MDM is just an app that can be wiped by "formatting" the device. In reality, management is tied to the **Hardware Serial Number** and **Apple's Activation Servers**, not just local software.

## Technical Workflow:

### 1. Setup Assistant (The Hello Screen)

After a DFU restore or factory reset, the device returns to the welcome screen. The device appears new, but its identity is still known to Apple.

### 2. Cloud Verification

When the device connects to Wi-Fi to activate, the iPad's underlying system reports its **Serial Number** to Apple's servers.

### 3. Forced Recall

Once Apple confirms the serial number belongs to a school via **Apple School Manager (ASM)**, it prevents the device from being set up as a personal device. It forces the device into the **Remote Management** screen.

### 4. Mandatory Enrollment

If the MDM profile is configured for "Mandatory Enrollment," the student cannot skip the setup step. Without school credentials, the device remains unusable.

## Strategy:

- **Data Disaster**: Restoring a device is an irreversible operation for local data. Students will **permanently lose** unsynced photos, notes, and schoolwork.
- **Maintenance Costs**: Failed restore attempts often leave devices in Recovery Mode, which still requires IT staff intervention. Unauthorized resets simply create unnecessary, avoidable work.
