**Yes, this is completely normal. It reflects a systematic shift in Apple's network security architecture designed to optimize connection speeds and ensure seamless compatibility with educational Content Delivery Networks (CDNs).**

In modern MDM deployments, to improve connection quality and reduce device battery drain, the security framework has transitioned from a traditional full-tunnel VPN to a more efficient **"DNS Proxy"** and **"Content Filter"** architecture.

## Key Changes:

1. **Icon Removal**: Because the system no longer creates a global or Per-App VPN tunnel, the "VPN" label is no longer displayed in the status bar. Please be assured this does **not** mean the device is unprotected.
2. **Improved Performance**: This new architecture significantly reduces background battery drain and resolves common issues such as network lag and app update failures.
3. **Seamless CDN Compatibility**: By moving away from a traditional VPN tunnel, devices can communicate directly and efficiently with local campus caching servers and educational CDNs, ensuring that heavy teaching content (like video lessons) loads at maximum speed.

## How to Verify Protection is Active:

- **Check the App**: Open the **Jamf Trust** app on the iPad. If you see a **Green Shield (Protected)**, the filtering and data reporting functions are operating correctly.
- **Inventory Status**: In the Jamf Pro dashboard, verify that the "Content Filter" and "DNS Proxy" configuration profiles are listed as "Installed" for that device.

## Strategy: Verifying Protection Status

If you see yellow or red system-level warnings inside the Jamf Trust app regarding "Passcode not set" or "OS outdated," you can usually ignore these as long as the main shield remains green and "Connected." These local warnings do not affect the core network filtering and usage data reporting functions. IT administrators can decide whether to enforce these local remediations based on internal school security policies.
