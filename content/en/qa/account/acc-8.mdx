**The recovery method depends on your Jamf Pro deployment model (Cloud vs. On-Premise) and whether Single Sign-On (SSO) is enabled.**

As the Digital Initiative wraps up in **August 2026**, vendor-supplied admin accounts may be deactivated. It is crucial that the school's internal IT team maintains secure, uninterrupted access to the highest-level administrator account.

## Scenario 1: Jamf Pro Cloud (Jamf Cloud)

1. **Use the Reset Link**:
   - Click **[Forgot password?]** on the Jamf Pro login screen.
   - Enter your administrator email address to trigger a password reset email.

2. **Ask Another Administrator**:
   - If other staff members have administrative privileges, ask them to log in and navigate to **[Settings]** > **[System Settings]** > **[Jamf Pro User Accounts & Groups]**.
   - They can select your account, click **[Edit]**, and manually assign you a new password.

3. **Contact Jamf Support**:
   - If you are the sole administrator and the reset email fails, log a support case via your **[Jamf Account](https://account.jamf.com)**.
   - Jamf support can assist in restoring access to the primary admin account after conducting a rigorous identity verification process.

## Scenario 2: SSO / Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA) is Enabled

If your Jamf Pro utilizes **Microsoft Entra ID** or **Google Workspace** for authentication:

- Password authority rests with the Identity Provider. You must reset your password directly within the Google or Microsoft portals, not on the Jamf Pro login page.

## Institutional Advice: Preparations for 2026

- **Create a 'Break-glass' Account**: Before the vendor hands over the system in **August 2026**, create a local administrator account that is **not** bound to a specific person's email and is excluded from SSO policies. Physically print these credentials and store them in a secure safe. This is your ultimate fallback if the SSO connection breaks or the primary admin leaves abruptly.
- **Redundant Admins**: Ensure the school always has at least two active personnel with administrative access to prevent a Single Point of Failure.
