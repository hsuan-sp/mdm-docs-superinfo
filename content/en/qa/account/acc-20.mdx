**Yes, absolutely. Managed Apple Accounts support robust MFA through either Apple's native managed recovery phone system or via delegated Federated Authentication.**

Given escalating cybersecurity threats, Apple enforces MFA as a non-negotiable standard for any account holding administrative privileges within ASM.

## The Two Methods of MFA Implementation:

### 1. Federated Authentication (Highly Recommended)

- If your ASM is federated with Google Workspace or Microsoft Entra ID, the entire MFA challenge and verification process is **offloaded to the Identity Provider (IdP)**.
- **The Workflow**: After entering their domain password, the user is prompted by Google Authenticator or Microsoft Authenticator (via push notification or SMS). Apple simply receives a secure token confirming successful authentication. This allows IT to manage all MFA policies and lockouts from a single, centralized Google or Microsoft dashboard.

### 2. Standard ASM Accounts (Non-Federated)

- During their initial login, users are required to register a trusted phone number to receive 6-digit verification codes via SMS or voice call.
- **Handling Lost Devices (Disaster Recovery)**: If a teacher loses their phone or changes their number and is locked out, personnel with the "Site Manager" or "Administrator" role must intervene:
  1. Log in to ASM and select the locked user from the **[Users]** list.
  2. In the right-hand details panel, locate the security section and click **[Reset MFA Status]**.
  3. This action flushes the old phone number from the system, prompting the user to register a new trusted device upon their next login attempt.

## Strategy: The Security Baseline

The responsibility for securing the MDM infrastructure falls entirely on the school, necessitating robust disaster recovery mechanisms.

- **Prevent Single Points of Failure**: It is a critical institutional mandate that all ASM accounts with the **[Administrator]** role must link at least two distinct recovery phone numbers (e.g., the IT Director's mobile and a fixed office line). Alternatively, deploy FIDO-compliant physical **Security Keys**. This ensures that if the primary administrator leaves abruptly, loses their phone, or is incapacitated, the school will not be permanently locked out of its own management servers.
